## 1. Dataset Scope

The evaluated dataset is `millat/StudyAbroadGPT-Dataset`, a synthetic, manually reviewed conversational dataset for study-abroad academic advising.

This report should be interpreted as a structural and procedural dataset-quality audit. It establishes formatting consistency, train/test separation, low duplication, and reproducible evaluation readiness. It does not, by itself, establish factual correctness, immigration-policy reliability, hallucination resistance, or expert-level educational advising accuracy.

## 4. Synthetic-Validity Paragraph for Paper

The StudyAbroadGPT-Dataset was developed as a synthetic domain-specific corpus because no open, labeled conversational dataset was available for study-abroad academic advising in low-resource contexts. Synthetic generation enabled coverage of recurring student questions across admissions, scholarships, visa preparation, accommodation, documentation, and academic planning, while manual review was used to improve clarity, remove irrelevant outputs, and reduce unsafe or unsupported advice. Approximately 200 conversations were manually inspected and corrected after synthetic generation. The dataset is therefore intended as an experimental fine-tuning resource rather than a replacement for official university or immigration sources. Its current validation supports structural quality, leakage control, preliminary topic coverage, and domain-adaptation utility; it does not claim exhaustive factual completeness or official policy reliability.

## 5. Recommended Topic Coverage Reporting

Use `outputs/topic_annotation_template.csv` to manually annotate 100–200 conversations. If time is limited, 100 carefully annotated samples are defensible.

In addition to the keyword-based topic analysis, `semantic_topic_classifier.py` provides an exploratory semantic topic classification using sentence-transformer similarity between the first user question and predefined topic descriptions. This reduced the `other/general advising` category from 32.0% under keyword matching to 11.0%, suggesting that keyword matching underclassified semantically related prompts. However, semantic classification should not be treated as ground-truth annotation: 96 of 200 assignments (48.0%) were flagged as low-confidence, and the classifier has not been validated against human-labeled topics. Therefore, the semantic topic distribution should be interpreted as preliminary dataset-characterization evidence rather than publication-grade supervised annotation.

The semantic classifier assigned 0.0% of the sampled conversations to `travel/pre-arrival preparation`. This result should be manually inspected before concluding that the category is absent from the dataset, because travel-related prompts may be semantically absorbed into broader categories such as student life, accommodation, or general advising.

## 7. Limitations Paragraph for Paper

The dataset is synthetic and was not validated against a comprehensive database of official university or immigration policies. Therefore, although it is useful for studying parameter-efficient domain adaptation, it should not be interpreted as a verified advising knowledge base. The current evidence primarily establishes structural and procedural quality rather than comprehensive knowledge quality. Future work should incorporate retrieval-augmented generation from official sources, source-grounded factuality auditing, multi-rater expert annotation, inter-rater agreement analysis, and bias analysis across destination countries, socioeconomic backgrounds, and applicant profiles.

## 8. Downstream Utility Framing

The downstream evaluation framework is intended to assess whether the LoRA-adapted model gains domain-specific utility over the base instruction model. Once base-model and LoRA-model responses are generated and scored for domain accuracy and helpfulness, the resulting comparison can provide task-level evidence for domain adaptation. Until those scores are completed, downstream utility should be treated as a planned evaluation rather than a demonstrated result.
