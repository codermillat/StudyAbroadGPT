# Dataset Factuality Error Catalog — gemini-1.0-pro Training Data (curated)

**What this is.** A source-verified factuality audit of the *training data itself*
— the synthetic study-abroad advising answers generated by **`gemini-1.0-pro`**
(hardcoded at `study_abroad_dataset/src/generator/conversation_generator.py:79`;
dataset generated in 2024). It applies the **same judgment-free, ground-truth
verification method** used in `Model_factuality_error_catalog.md`: each contested
claim is checked against an authoritative external source (cited), independent of
any model's judgment.

**Why this exists.** The model audit established that the LoRA fine-tuned model
produced four hard factual errors that the base model did not. That alone cannot
distinguish *method* failure from *data* failure. This catalog closes the causal
loop by auditing whether those same errors were **already present in the training
distribution**. If they were, the fine-tuning faithfully reproduced training-set
errors — i.e., the **data pipeline is the cause, and the adapter-handoff method is
sound** (it learned the distribution it was given, as designed).

**Generator provenance.** `model_name="gemini-1.0-pro"` — a December-2023-era
model, materially weaker than the 1.5/2.x generation. The dataset was built in
2024 by a `{program} × {university}` template cross-product (`config.py`), which
mechanically produced **factually incoherent prompts** (e.g., "MS in Data Science
at Harvard Medical School," "Bachelor of Medicine at Oxford" applied as a generic
template). The generator answered these as if valid rather than rejecting the
false premise — the precise failure mode the fine-tuned model later inherited.

## The causal match: each model error traced to the training data

For all four model errors in `Model_factuality_error_catalog.md`, the **same class
of false claim is present in the gemini-1.0-pro training answers.**

### D-Case 1 — Australia healthcare: false Medicare eligibility (matches Model Case 2) — SEVERITY: HIGH
- **Training prompt:** "Are there affordable healthcare options for Pakistani students in Australia?"
- **Training answer (WRONG):** Lists "Medicare" as an option and states "Pakistani students who have been living in Australia for more than 12 months may be eligible" for Medicare.
- **Ground truth:** International students are **not** eligible for Medicare (save limited Reciprocal Health Care Agreement countries — Pakistan/Nepal are **not** among them); **OSHC is compulsory** under student-visa condition 8501. ([Study Australia (gov)](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/overseas-student-health-cover-oshc); [privatehealth.gov.au](https://www.privatehealth.gov.au/health_insurance/overseas/overseas_student_health_cover.htm))
- **Match:** The model's sample-11 error ("eligible for Medicare … after at least six months") is a near-verbatim reproduction of this training-set falsehood. **Smoking gun.**

### D-Case 2 — Brazil→Bangladesh scholarships: fabricated programs (matches Model Case 4) — SEVERITY: HIGH
- **Training prompt:** "What scholarships are available for Brazilian students in Bangladesh?"
- **Training answer (WRONG):** "Bangladesh Commonwealth Scholarships … offered by the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission to students from Commonwealth countries, **including Brazil**"; also invents a "Bangabandhu Scholarship" for this corridor.
- **Ground truth:** **Brazil is not a Commonwealth member**, so a Brazilian citizen is ineligible for Commonwealth Scholarships. ([British Council — Commonwealth Scholarships](https://study-uk.britishcouncil.org/scholarships-funding/commonwealth-scholarships))
- **Match:** The model's sample-34 fabrication of specific Brazil→Bangladesh scholarship programs reproduces the training data's fabrication pattern.

### D-Case 3 — "Bachelor of Medicine at Oxford": false-premise elaboration (matches Model Case 3, Stanford) — SEVERITY: MED
- **Training prompt:** "What is the eligibility criteria for Bachelor of Medicine at Oxford?" (a template artifact — Oxford's degree is the BM BCh, and the "Bachelor of Medicine" label was cross-applied to many universities).
- **Training answer (WRONG):** Confidently elaborates a "Bachelor of Medicine program" and fabricates a specific bar ("in 2023, Oxford required A* grades in Biology, Chemistry, and either Physics or Mathematics").
- **Ground truth:** Oxford Medicine (A100) requires A*AA with Chemistry **and** one of Biology/Physics/Mathematics — not three A*s across those subjects; the "Bachelor of Medicine" framing is a template artifact, not Oxford's actual program name.
- **Match:** Same false-premise-acceptance behavior as the model's Stanford "Bachelor of Medicine" error (sample 37). The behavior was trained in.

### D-Case 4 — "MS in Data Science at Harvard Medical School": incoherent program accepted (matches Model Case 1) — SEVERITY: MED
- **Training prompt:** "Do I need to submit my HSC for admission to MS in Data Science at Harvard Medical School?" (template cross-product; HMS offers no such program).
- **Training answer (WRONG):** Answers as if the program exists, fabricating HSC admission requirements rather than flagging that HMS has no MS in Data Science.
- **Ground truth:** Harvard Medical School does not offer an "MS in Data Science"; the prompt is incoherent and the correct response is to reject the premise.
- **Match:** Same category error as the model's Harvard-Med GRE / Stanford errors — accept the false premise and confabulate specifics.

## Mechanism (root cause)

1. **Weak generator.** `gemini-1.0-pro` (2023) has limited factual reliability on
   long-tail, policy-sensitive advising facts.
2. **Template cross-product prompting.** `{program} × {university}` generation
   produced incoherent prompts; the generator elaborated rather than rejecting
   false premises.
3. **No source verification in the pipeline.** Synthetic answers were accepted
   without ground-truth checking (the `quality_validator` checks structure/length,
   not factual correctness).
4. **Faithful fine-tuning.** QLoRA did exactly what it should: it raised fidelity
   to the training distribution (BERTScore +0.063). Because that distribution
   carried the errors above, fidelity and factual reliability moved in **opposite**
   directions.

**Conclusion.** The four model errors are not method artifacts — each is present,
in the same form, in the gemini-1.0-pro training data. The adapter-handoff
fine-tuning method is sound; the **synthetic-data pipeline is the locus of
failure.** This is the transferable caution: fine-tuning on unverified
LLM-generated data inherits the generator's errors, and standard distribution-
fidelity metrics (BERTScore/ROUGE) will *reward* that inheritance.
